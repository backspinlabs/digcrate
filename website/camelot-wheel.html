The Camelot wheel, explained

The Camelot wheel gives each of the 24 major and minor keys a short code, from 1A to 12B, so you can find a next track that mixes in key without working out the theory each time.

Camelot is a trademark of Mixed In Key LLC, which uses the wheel in its key-detection software and publishes the official chart. This page describes the system as Mixed In Key documents it.

How the codes work

  • The letter is the mode. A is minor and B is major.
  • The number is the position on the wheel. Neighboring numbers are a fifth apart, so their key signatures differ by one sharp or flat. Going up one number is going up a fifth: 8B is C major and 9B is G major.
  • The same number pairs a minor key with its relative major. 8A (A minor) and 8B (C major) share a key signature.
  • The wheel wraps. 12 sits next to 1, so 12A to 1A is a one-step move like any other.

Moves that mix cleanly

From any code, three moves keep you in a closely related key. Here they are from 8A, A minor.

Mixed In Key also describes deliberate jumps. Adding two to the number and keeping the letter (8A to 10A) moves the key up a whole step; it calls this an energy boost, and notes that a key change alone does not guarantee a rise in energy. Its guide is plain about the limit of the wheel: “Use the wheel to shortlist tracks. Phrasing, tempo, vocals and basslines still decide whether the blend works.”

Close in pitch, far on the wheel

The wheel is ordered by fifths, not by pitch. A key one semitone higher is seven steps around: 8A (A minor) to 3A (B♭ minor), or 8B (C major) to 3B (D♭ major). Keys a semitone apart share few notes, so they usually clash in a blend.

That matters when you change tempo. Without key lock, playing a track about 6 percent faster raises it one semitone, which moves it seven steps on the wheel. Use key lock (Master Tempo in Rekordbox) when you pitch a track to match, or work out its new code.

Seeing codes in your DJ software

Rekordbox can show keys as alphanumeric codes such as 1A and 2A instead of key names. Open Preferences > View > Display Type and set Key display format to Alphanumeric. The Rekordbox key-order guide covers sorting a playlist by key and importing a planned set.

Using the wheel with DigCrate

DigCrate is a Mac app that reads the BPM, key, and energy of your local library and shows each key in this 8A-style code. When it plans a set, every handoff is scored on the harmonic wheel, with a short note on why it works or clashes, and weak transitions are flagged with the BPM, key, and energy of a bridge track that would fix them.
